      We are more interested in recommending “books you will love” rather than “books you will like”.

      For this reason, we build our recommendations on top of deliberately-curated lists in the style of “my ten favorite novels of all time”. Hundreds of authors and critics have submitted similar lists to various publications. Any aggregation of these lists tends to converge on a small canon that “averages out” any unique tastes. Thankfully, many publications have been generous enough to publish the individual voter lists that make up their aggregate rankings.

      “On the Same Page” aims to match you with individuals who share your unique preferences. As you might expect from the above description of our underlying data — with apologies to the less pretentious among you — we skew toward “the classics”.

      A few tips:

      1. Enter books you truly love; perhaps those that would belong on your “top ten” list.
      2. Our engine works best with 3+ of your own inputs. It also works best with a mix of more popular and more distinct inputs.
      3. Expand any recommended result to see the voters’ lists that drive that recommendation.
      4. Manipulate the “Popular / Distinctive” slider to steer your recommendations towards, well, more popular or more distinct results.
        1. Note that pure popularity (co-occurrence) would skew towards recommending the most frequently-occurring books, almost regardless of inputs.
        2. It defaults to the setting we have found most helpful.
