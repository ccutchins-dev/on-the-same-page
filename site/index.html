      About

      How to Use

      1. Enter the books you love.
        • The method works best with four or more books.
        • It also works best with a mix of the relatively popular and relatively rare (among our dataset) books.
      2. Use the slider to bias results toward more popular associations with your books, or rarer ones.
        • It defaults to the setting we've found most useful.
        • Read more about the Distinctiveness vs. Popularity slider in the Methodology section below.
      3. Expand any entry in the results to see why that book was matched to yours.

      The Underlying Data

      Several publications have asked authors and critics for their ten favorite novels of all time. We find these deliberate lists of "books I absolutely love" far more meaningful than more thorough ratings of every book someone has ever read.

      Methodology

      Those same publications tend to blend the individual lists into one overall ranking. Your good friends at On the Same Page do the opposite: we match you with the individual contributors who share your taste.

      Your input books are compared against every contributor's list, and candidate books are scored on two signals:

      • Popularity (co-occurrence): how often a book appears on the same lists as the ones you chose. This leans toward widely loved, frequently listed books, no matter your inputs.
      • Distinctiveness: how strongly a book is tied to your choices beyond what its general popularity would predict. This surfaces books that are specifically meaningful to readers like you, rather than books that simply appear everywhere.

      The slider manipulates the strength that these two signals are given in defining a single ranking.
